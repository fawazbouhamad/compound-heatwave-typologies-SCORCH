DBSCAN NEIGHBOURHOOD-DISTANCE COMPARISON

What was tested?
----------------
This comparison tested two lists of candidate neighbourhood distances
within DBSCAN on the same 395 regionally extensive days. Both methods use
DBSCAN; the difference is which distances are included in the search.

The two settings
----------------
Epsilon (eps) is the distance defining a cell's neighbourhood. Min_samples
is the number of cells needed in that neighbourhood, including the cell
itself, for it to be a core cell. Connected core neighbourhoods form clusters,
and nearby border cells can join them. Other cells are labelled as noise.

Distances here are Euclidean distances in 1-degree grid-cell units, not km.

Method A uses evenly spaced distances:
  1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4

Method B uses distances between cell centres on the square grid:
  1, sqrt(2), 2, sqrt(5), 2*sqrt(2), 3, sqrt(10), sqrt(13), 4

For example, sqrt(2) reaches a diagonal neighbour, while sqrt(5) is the
distance across two grid steps in one direction and one in the other.

Both lists were crossed with min_samples = 4, 5, ..., 12. This gives 63
candidate pairs per day for A and 81 for B. The selection rule retains pairs
giving the most frequent nonzero cluster count. Tied most-frequent counts
are pooled. Epsilon is averaged over the retained pairs, and min_samples is
averaged and rounded to the nearest integer, with halves rounded upward.

What the file shows
-------------------
overall_method_comparison_summary.csv summarizes agreement across the 395
days. This was a comparison of cell groupings, before ellipse fitting.

- The same number of clusters was found on 92.4% of days.
- The complete grouping of cells was identical on 75.2% of days.
- The adjusted Rand index (ARI) was at least 0.90 on 89.4% of days.
- Median ARI, normalized mutual information (NMI), and matched-cluster
  intersection over union (IoU) were all 1.00.
- The median distance between matched cluster centroids was 0 km.
- Neither method produced a day with zero clusters.

ARI and NMI compare how cells are grouped, allowing cluster names to differ.
IoU measures shared cells divided by all cells in either matched cluster.
A value of 1 indicates full agreement for each of these measures. Noise
means cells left outside clusters; the table includes agreement measures
with noise included and with cells labelled as noise by both methods removed.

The baseline columns compare with contiguous, diagonally connected groups.
"Splitting" and "merging" describe differences from that reference; they
are not counts of known mistakes. High median agreement also does not mean
the two methods gave identical results on every day.

Choice used in the study and why
-------------------------------
The manuscript uses Method A: distances from 1 to 4 in steps of 0.5, with
min_samples from 4 to 12. This is a simple, evenly spaced search to describe
and apply. The saved comparison shows broad agreement with the alternative
grid, supporting its use without establishing that one grid is always best.

In the final workflow, consecutive selected days share the largest daily
epsilon and min_samples chosen within that sequence. The distance-grid
comparison here is the earlier daily sensitivity check.

REGIONAL-DAY THRESHOLD COMPARISON

What was tested?
----------------
This comparison checked how strict the selection of regionally extensive
heatwave days should be. It counted heatwave grid cells on each of the
15,738 April-September days from 1940 to 2025, including days with no
heatwave cells, and tested cutoffs in that distribution of daily counts.

This threshold is separate from the local 95th-percentile temperature
threshold used to identify heatwaves at individual grid cells.

Methods
-------
Three regional percentiles were tested: the 95th, 97.5th and 99th. A higher
percentile keeps fewer days with more widespread heatwave conditions.

Each percentile was calculated in three ways:
- higher: use the observed count at the upper of the two neighbouring
  positions around the percentile rank.
- nearest: use the observed count at the nearest position.
- linear: interpolate between the neighbouring counts. This can give a
  fractional cutoff even though the number of cells is an integer.

A day is selected when its heatwave-cell count is at least the cutoff.

What the file shows
-------------------
regional_day_selection_sensitivity.csv has nine rows, one for each
percentile/method combination. It records the cutoff, number and fraction
of selected days, and their mean, maximum and minimum heatwave-cell counts.
The column names containing "circles" refer to grid cells in the earlier
maps, not to ellipses or events.

The saved results are:

97.5th       all three                   371.00             395

At the selected 97.5th percentile, all three calculation methods agree on
the cutoff of 371 cells. The 395 retained days account for about 2.51% of
the warm-season dates. Ties in integer counts mean the retained fraction
does not have to be exactly 2.5%.

Choice used in the study and why
-------------------------------
The study uses the 97.5th percentile with the higher method: at least 371
of the region's 1,800 cells must be experiencing a heatwave on a selected
day. This is about 20.6% of the grid cells, not an area-weighted percentage.

This choice focuses the analysis on widespread days while retaining more
days than the 99th-percentile cutoff. The higher method also gives an
observed integer count. Agreement among all three methods at 97.5% shows
that this particular cutoff is insensitive to the interpolation choice.
It is a study-design choice; this table does not identify a uniquely optimal
percentile. The selected days become Catalog 2 and feed the clustering step.

LGCP TEMPERATURE-COVARIATE COMPARISON

What was tested?
----------------
A log-Gaussian Cox process (LGCP) models where points are more concentrated,
while allowing additional spatial variation beyond the measured covariates.
Here, the points are the 760 heatwave-cluster centroids of the historical first analysis (the final analysis has 753).

This earlier comparison tested four combinations of temperature predictors.
Every model included longitude and latitude, used the same centroids and
1,800-cell study grid, and was fitted by minimum contrast using the K
statistic and an exponential spatial covariance model.

Temperature measures and models
-------------------------------
The temperature measures summarize daily Tmax during April-September
1940-2025 at each grid cell:
- Mean Tmax: the average temperature, describing typical warm-season heat.
- Standard deviation (SD): the spread around that average, in degrees Celsius.
- Coefficient of variation (CV): SD divided by the mean, describing spread
  relative to the mean used in this calculation.

The archived comparison used standardized temperature predictors, marked
with "_z". Each was centred and scaled across the 1,800 grid cells so that
its spatial mean was zero and its population standard deviation was one.

Model label   Temperature predictors (longitude and latitude always included)
baseline      Mean Tmax + CV
variant1      Mean Tmax only
variant2      CV only
variant3      Mean Tmax + SD

The models therefore test whether to include both temperature level and
variability, and whether variability is expressed as CV or SD.

Files and findings
------------------
- tmax_covariate_correlations.csv compares the predictors. Pearson measures
  linear association; Spearman compares their ranks. The Pearson correlation
  of mean Tmax with CV is -0.598, compared with -0.220 for mean Tmax with SD.
  CV and SD are strongly related to each other (0.889).
- lgcp_model_parameters.csv stores the fitted trend coefficients and spatial
  covariance parameters for all four models. The standardized mean-Tmax
  coefficient is positive whenever it is included. The SD coefficient in
  variant3 is small (-0.0072); this alone is not a significance test.
- grid_predicted_intensity_all_variants.csv contains the predictors and four
  fitted intensity values for each grid cell. Values ending in "x1e6" are
  multiplied by one million for readability. Intensity describes expected
  centroid density per unit area, not the probability of a heatwave.
- figures/panel_model_comparison.png compares the four spatial patterns.
- spatial_risk_lgcp_model_diagnostics.R fits the alternatives and writes
  diagnostics. spatial_risk_lgcp_figures_final.R draws the comparison from
  saved coefficients. Their default paths refer to the earlier workspace;
  they are not steps in the current manuscript reproduction.

These are archived fits. The current manuscript model uses mean Tmax and SD
in their original temperature units, so its coefficients should be read
from the current model outputs. This folder does not contain a formal test
establishing that one of the four models predicts best.

Choice used in the study and why
-------------------------------
The study uses mean Tmax and its standard deviation, together with longitude
and latitude: the predictor combination called variant3 in this comparison.

Mean Tmax describes temperature level, while SD describes absolute
temperature variability. This keeps both quantities directly interpretable
in degrees Celsius. The saved correlations also show less overlap between
mean Tmax and SD than between mean Tmax and CV. These points support the
choice, without implying that SD was proved to improve predictive accuracy.
The final LGCP is fitted using all 753 centroids.

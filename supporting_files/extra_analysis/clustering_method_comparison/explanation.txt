CLUSTERING METHOD COMPARISON

What was tested?
----------------
This comparison examined ways to group heatwave grid cells into spatial
clusters on the same 395 regionally extensive days. The question was how
the grouping method affects the clusters retained for ellipse fitting.

Methods
-------
1. Connected components (baseline): cells belong to the same component if
   they can be linked through touching heatwave cells, including diagonal
   neighbours. The earlier workflow then merged nearby components and
   selected sufficiently large, distant secondary components.
2. DBSCAN: groups cells through dense neighbourhoods and can leave isolated
   cells as noise. This historical test used a neighbourhood radius of 1.5
   grid-cell units and min_samples = 3.
3. OPTICS: orders cells according to density-based reachability, then extracts
   clusters from changes in that ordering. This test used min_samples = 3
   and xi = 0.05, which controls the steepness used to identify clusters.
4. HDBSCAN: examines density clustering across distance scales and selects
   stable groups. This test used min_cluster_size = 5 and min_samples = 3.

The density methods used Euclidean distances in grid-cell units, not
kilometres. Small or nested clusters could be absorbed or excluded by the
earlier selection rules. All methods then used the same PCA ellipse
calculation, Tmax-weighted centroid shift, and fixed ellipse scale of 1.25.

What the file shows
-------------------
method_summary.csv contains one row per method, with averages over the 395
days. In this file, a "reportable blob" means a cluster retained after those
earlier selection rules.

Useful columns are:
- raw_cluster_count: clusters before the selection rules.
- reportable_blob_count and reportable_cell_share: retained clusters and
  the fraction of heatwave cells they contain.
- suppressed_* and absorbed_cluster_count: excluded or combined clusters.
- noise_or_other_cells: cells outside the retained clusters; these are not
  necessarily all cells labelled as noise by the clustering algorithm.
- reportable_ari_vs_baseline: agreement of the cell groupings with the
  baseline, measured by the adjusted Rand index (ARI). A value of 1 means
  identical groupings, regardless of cluster names.
- centroid_shift_vs_baseline_km: separation between the weighted centroids
  of the largest retained cluster from each method and the baseline.

The other columns summarize ellipse size, shape, orientation and coverage.
The baseline is a reference for comparison, not an independently known truth.

Saved findings
--------------
                         Baseline   DBSCAN   OPTICS   HDBSCAN
Mean retained clusters       1.13     2.13     2.30      2.22
Mean retained cell share    84.9%    97.9%    97.5%     97.5%
Mean ARI against baseline    1.000    0.710    0.664     0.645

Among the density methods, DBSCAN had the highest average agreement with
the baseline and the smallest average shift of the largest-cluster centroid
(77.6 km). These results describe the tested settings, not every possible
setting of each algorithm. They also use an earlier workflow and should not
be read as counts or validation of the final 753 clusters.

Choice used in the study and why
-------------------------------
The study uses DBSCAN. It does not require the number of clusters in advance,
can identify irregular spatial groups, and can separate low-density cells
as noise. The saved comparison also shows that it retained most heatwave
cells while remaining relatively close to the baseline grouping.

The final analysis selects DBSCAN parameters from a daily parameter search;
it does not use the fixed 1.5/3 setting from this early comparison. The
neighbouring dbscan_eps_grid_comparison folder explains that separate test.
